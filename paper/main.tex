\begin{abstract}
Programmatic real-time bidding (RTB) requires demand-side platforms (DSPs) to control ad spend across multi-day campaigns. Recent programmatic exchanges replaced Generalized Second-Price (GSP) auctions with First-Price Auctions (FPA). In FPA, winning advertisers pay their exact bid. This transition removes the second-price clearing buffer and increases financial risk from poor pacing. Standard heuristics, such as Uniform Linear Pacing (ULP) and Proportional-Integral-Derivative (PID) controllers, frequently cause early budget depletion or under-delivery under auction volatility. 

In this paper, we propose an end-to-end budget pacing framework based on Temporal Fusion Transformers (TFT). The framework unifies budget control for both FPA and GSP markets. The architecture uses Gated Residual Networks (GRN) to stabilize training, Variable Selection Networks (VSN) to filter noisy inputs, Multi-Head Attention to learn periodic patterns, and a linear autoregressive skip connection to anchor local spend levels. The model forecasts multi-quantile spend trajectories $\hat{y}_{t+\tau}(q)$ for $q \in \{0.10, 0.50, 0.90\}$ over a 24-hour horizon. We integrate these forecasts into a closed-form throttling policy that adjusts outgoing bid multipliers $\alpha_t$. We prove downside budget solvency mathematically for both auction mechanisms. In addition, we design a dual-boundary spike detector that separates market shocks from regular traffic peaks. Empirical tests on 19.5 million iPinYou auctions show that our framework achieves an out-of-sample MASE of $0.1465$. The policy achieves a $94.87\%$ budget utilization rate without premature depletion ($0.0\%$), and reduces pacing volatility to $0.0622$ ($62\%$ lower than PID control).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Real-time bidding (RTB) handles billions of ad impressions daily~\cite{zhang2014real}. In recent years, programmatic ad exchanges transitioned from Generalized Second-Price (GSP) auctions to First-Price Auctions (FPA) to improve pricing transparency~\cite{despotakis2021first,zhou2021bid}. In second-price auctions, the second-highest bid determines the clearing price. In first-price auctions, winning bidders pay their submitted bid. This market shift eliminates the clearing price buffer and increases financial penalties for incorrect pacing.

Modern Demand-Side Platforms (DSPs) separate real-time bidding into two operational stages:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Micro-Level Bid Shading:} Predictive models shade bid valuations at millisecond intervals to maximize surplus against estimated clearing prices~\cite{zhou2021bid,balseiro2021budget}.
    \item \textbf{Macro-Level Budget Pacing:} Pacing algorithms adjust bid multipliers at hourly intervals to distribute campaign budget $B$ across campaign lifespan $T$~\cite{xu2015smart,grigas2017profit}.
\end{enumerate}
Without macro-pacing, campaigns deplete their budget during morning traffic surges. The campaign then stops before high-value evening hours. Conversely, overly conservative bidding fails to spend the allocated budget and reduces ad reach~\cite{bhalgat2012online}. In first-price auctions, spend scales linearly with submitted bids. Volatile pacing multipliers directly cause unstable financial exposure.

Standard pacing methods have clear operational limitations:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Uniform Linear Pacing (ULP)~\cite{xu2015smart}:} Allocates a constant spend rate $B/T$ to each hour. This method ignores daily circadian cycles and weekend traffic variations.
    \item \textbf{PID Feedback Control~\cite{lee2013real}:} Adjusts bid multipliers based on accumulated spending errors. PID controllers suffer from phase lag and oscillate during sudden traffic spikes.
    \item \textbf{Lagged Decision Trees (GBDT)~\cite{ke2017lightgbm}:} Autoregressive models (e.g., LightGBM) predict point estimates with recursive steps. They do not model multi-horizon joint distributions and lack calibrated uncertainty intervals.
\end{enumerate}

To resolve these problems, we formulate budget pacing as a multi-horizon forecasting problem with a risk-averse control policy. We propose a pacing framework that uses \textbf{Temporal Fusion Transformers (TFT)}~\cite{lim2021temporal}. TFT processes static campaign metadata, past auction metrics, and known calendar features. TFT outputs calibrated multi-quantile predictions ($\hat{y}_{t+\tau}(0.10), \hat{y}_{t+\tau}(0.50), \hat{y}_{t+\tau}(0.90)$). Our throttling policy uses these quantiles to protect against auction volatility.

We make five primary contributions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Unified Auction Pacing Framework:} We formulate a pacing policy that operates across both First-Price Auctions (with bid shading) and Generalized Second-Price auctions.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Horizon TFT Architecture:} We construct a TFT model with a linear autoregressive skip connection, a 168-hour lookback, and a 24-hour forecast horizon. Gated Residual Networks (GRN) stabilize training, and Variable Selection Networks (VSN) filter input noise.
    \item \textbf{Quantile Throttling Policy and Solvency Proof:} We derive a closed-form bid multiplier $\alpha_{t+1}$ using upper quantile spend forecasts $\sum \hat{y}(0.90)$. We prove budget solvency for both FPA and SPA mechanisms.
    \item \textbf{Dual-Boundary Spike Detector:} We implement an adaptive detector that compares realized spend against conditional quantiles. This detector separates external market shocks from predictable daily traffic peaks.
    \item \textbf{Empirical Evaluation on RTB Data:} We evaluate our framework on 19.5 million iPinYou auctions with chronological data splits. The model achieves an out-of-sample MASE of $0.1465$, $94.87\%$ budget utilization, $0.0\%$ premature depletion, and a PVI of $0.0622$ ($62\%$ lower volatility than PID control).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Unified Auction Mechanics: FPA and SPA}
Consider a DSP that manages $M$ active campaigns across programmatic exchanges. Exchanges operate under First-Price Auctions (FPA) or Generalized Second-Price (GSP / SPA) rules. When an ad opportunity $x$ arrives at hour $t$, the valuation engine computes impression value $v(x) = \text{eCPM} \cdot \text{pCTR}(x)$. To balance immediate surplus with campaign budget constraints, the bidding engine calculates final bid $b_t(x)$ using bid multiplier $\alpha_t^{(i)} \in [\alpha_{\min}, 1.0]$:
\begin{equation}
    b_t(x) = \begin{cases}
        \alpha_t^{(i)} \cdot \beta\big(v(x)\big), & \text{in First-Price Auctions (FPA)} \\
        \alpha_t^{(i)} \cdot v(x), & \text{in Second-Price Auctions (SPA)}
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:bidding_mechanisms}
\end{equation}
where $\beta(v(x)) \le v(x)$ represents a micro-level bid shading policy~\cite{zhou2021bid,balseiro2021budget}. 

When campaign $c_i$ wins opportunity $x \in \mathcal{W}_t^{(i)}$, the exchange charges clearing price $p(x)$:
\begin{equation}
    p(x) = \begin{cases}
        b_t(x), & \text{in FPA (Pay-as-Bid)} \\
        \max_{j \ne i} b_t^{(j)}(x) \le b_t(x), & \text{in SPA (Second-Price)}
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Total campaign spend during hour $t$ is:
\begin{equation}
    y_t^{(i)} = \sum_{x \in \mathcal{W}_t^{(i)}} p(x)
\end{equation}

\textbf{Operational Implication:} In second-price auctions, the second-highest bid limits clearing expenditure. This mechanism provides a buffer against aggressive pacing. In first-price auctions, spend $y_t^{(i)}$ is linear in bid $b_t(x)$. Over-pacing drains capital directly with zero market buffer. Therefore, first-price pacing requires calibrated downside risk bounds.

\subsection{Optimization Objective}
Let $S_t^{(i)} = \sum_{\tau=1}^t y_\tau^{(i)}$ represent cumulative spend through hour $t$. The campaign ends at hour $T$. The DSP aims to maximize total ad utility while satisfying three operational constraints:
\begin{align}
    \text{maximize} \quad & \sum_{t=1}^T \sum_{x \in \mathcal{W}_t^{(i)}} \mathbb{E}[\text{Utility}(x)] \\
    \text{subject to} \quad & S_T^{(i)} \le B^{(i)} \quad \text{(Budget Solvency)} \label{eq:budget_constraint} \\
    & S_t^{(i)} < B^{(i)}, \; \forall t < T - \delta \quad \text{(Anti-Depletion)} \label{eq:depletion_constraint} \\
    & \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T-1} (\alpha_{t+1}^{(i)} - \alpha_t^{(i)})^2 \le \epsilon \quad \text{(Pacing Smoothness)} \label{eq:smoothness_constraint}
\end{align}
Constraint~(\ref{eq:budget_constraint}) prevents budget overspending. Constraint~(\ref{eq:depletion_constraint}) prevents early budget depletion. Constraint~(\ref{eq:smoothness_constraint}) limits the Pacing Volatility Index ($PVI$).

\subsection{Quantile-Hedging Throttling Policy}
Given forecast trajectory $\hat{y}_{t+\tau}^{(i)}(q)$ for $\tau \in \{1, \dots, \tau_{\max}\}$, we define the update rule for multiplier $\alpha_{t+1}^{(i)}$:
\begin{equation}
    \alpha_{t+1}^{(i)} = \text{clip}\left(\alpha_t^{(i)} \cdot \frac{B^{(i)} - S_t^{(i)}}{\sum_{\tau=1}^{\tau_{\max}} \hat{y}_{t+\tau}^{(i)}(0.90)}, \; \alpha_{\min}, \; 1.0\right)
    \label{eq:pacing_policy}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha_{\min} = 0.05$ prevents bidding shutoff.

\begin{lemma}[Downside Budget Solvency Across FPA and SPA]
Let $R_t^{(i)} = B^{(i)} - S_t^{(i)}$ be the remaining budget reserve. Assume market spend satisfies $y_{t+\tau}^{(i)} \le \hat{y}_{t+\tau}^{(i)}(0.90)$ for each hour $\tau \in [1, \tau_{\max}]$. Under update rule~(\ref{eq:pacing_policy}) without multiplier clipping, cumulative expenditure over the 24-hour horizon satisfies:
\begin{equation}
    \sum_{\tau=1}^{\tau_{\max}} y_{t+\tau}^{(i)} \le R_t^{(i)}
\end{equation}
This bound guarantees budget solvency for both FPA and SPA mechanisms.
\end{lemma}

In FPA, clearing price satisfies $p(x) = b(x) = \alpha \cdot \beta(v(x))$. Cumulative spend under multiplier $\alpha_{t+1}$ scales linearly: $Y_{t, \tau_{\max}}(\alpha_{t+1}) = \frac{\alpha_{t+1}}{\alpha_t} Y_{t, \tau_{\max}}(\alpha_t)$. In SPA, spend scales monotonically with $\alpha_{t+1}$ because $p(x) \le b(x)$. Under condition $y_{t+\tau} \le \hat{y}_{t+\tau}(0.90)$, realized spend satisfies $Y_{t, \tau_{\max}}(\alpha_{t+1}) \le \frac{\alpha_{t+1}}{\alpha_t} \sum_{\tau=1}^{\tau_{\max}} \hat{y}_{t+\tau}^{(i)}(0.90)$. Substituting the policy definition yields:
\begin{equation}
    Y_{t, \tau_{\max}}(\alpha_{t+1}) \le \left(\frac{\alpha_t \cdot \frac{R_t^{(i)}}{\sum \hat{y}(0.90)}}{\alpha_t}\right) \cdot \sum_{\tau=1}^{\tau_{\max}} \hat{y}_{t+\tau}^{(i)}(0.90) = R_t^{(i)}
\end{equation}
Therefore, realized spend cannot exceed remaining reserves, which guarantees solvency under both auction rules.

\subsection{Feature Schema and Input Representation}
The model processes three input feature groups:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Static Context Vector ($\mathbf{s}^{(i)}$):} Fixed campaign metadata, including \texttt{campaign\_id}, total budget $B^{(i)}$, duration $T$, nominal velocity $B^{(i)}/T$, and industry vertical embeddings.
    \item \textbf{Observed Past Inputs ($\mathbf{z}_t^{(i)} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_z}$):} Historical metrics logged over lookback window $[t-k+1, t]$ ($k=168$ hours): hourly spend $y_t$, impressions won $I_t$, win rate $w_t = I_t / (\text{bid requests})$, average CPM, and spend ratio $S_t / B$.
    \item \textbf{Known Future Inputs ($\mathbf{x}_t^{(i)} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_x}$):} Deterministic covariates known across forecast horizon $[t+1, t+\tau_{\max}]$ ($\tau_{\max}=24$ hours): cyclical hour terms $\sin(2\pi \cdot \text{hour}/24)$ and $\cos(2\pi \cdot \text{hour}/24)$, day-of-week embeddings, weekend flags, and countdown ratio $(T - t)/T$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Gated Residual Networks (GRN)}
Auction signals vary across peak and off-peak hours. Standard deep networks easily overfit or encounter vanishing gradients during low-volume hours. We use Gated Residual Networks (GRN) to provide non-linear transformations with identity shortcuts:
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{GRN}_\omega(\mathbf{a}, \mathbf{c}) = \mathrm{LayerNorm}\left(\mathbf{a} + \mathrm{GLU}_\omega\left(\mathbf{W}_{1}\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{W}_{2}\mathbf{c} + \mathbf{b}_{1}\right)\right)
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{a}$ is the primary feature vector, $\mathbf{c}$ is an optional static conditioning vector, $\mathrm{LayerNorm}(\cdot)$ is layer normalization, and $\mathrm{GLU}_\omega(\boldsymbol{\gamma})$ is the Gated Linear Unit:
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{GLU}_\omega(\boldsymbol{\gamma}) = \sigma\left(\mathbf{W}_3 \boldsymbol{\gamma} + \mathbf{b}_3\right) \odot \left(\mathbf{W}_4 \boldsymbol{\gamma} + \mathbf{b}_4\right)
\end{equation}
where $\sigma(\cdot)$ is the sigmoid activation function and $\odot$ denotes the element-wise product. When $\sigma(\cdot) \to 0$, the gate bypasses the non-linear transformation.

\subsection{Variable Selection Networks (VSN)}
Raw auction logs contain noisy features. We use Variable Selection Networks (VSN) to select the most informative features at each time step. For a set of transformed feature vectors $\{\boldsymbol{\xi}_t^{(j)}\}_{j=1}^{m}$, the model computes feature weights $\mathbf{v}_t \in \mathbb{R}^m$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{v}_t = \mathrm{Softmax}\left(\mathrm{GRN}_{v}\left([\boldsymbol{\xi}_t^{(1)}, \dots, \boldsymbol{\xi}_t^{(m)}], \mathbf{c}_s\right)\right)
\end{equation}
The aggregated feature vector is the weighted combination:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_t = \sum_{j=1}^{m} v_{t, j} \cdot \mathrm{GRN}_{\xi}^{(j)}\left(\boldsymbol{\xi}_t^{(j)}\right)
\end{equation}

\subsection{Interpretable Multi-Head Self-Attention}
To model long-range temporal relations without losing multi-day diurnal periodicity, the sequence passes through an interpretable multi-head attention layer over lookback $k=168$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{A}(\mathbf{Q}, \mathbf{K}, \mathbf{V}) = \mathrm{Softmax}\left(\frac{\mathbf{Q} \mathbf{K}^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)\mathbf{V}
\end{equation}
The model shares value projections across attention heads. The attention weights show the temporal importance of preceding hours directly, allowing engineers to verify 24-hour and 168-hour cyclic patterns.

\subsection{Multi-Quantile Loss Formulation}
We forecast three quantiles $\mathcal{Q} = \{0.10, 0.50, 0.90\}$ across all horizon steps $\tau \in \{1, \dots, \tau_{\max}\}$. We train the model by minimizing the Quantile Pinball Loss:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{TFT}} = \frac{1}{N \tau_{\max}} \sum_{i=1}^N \sum_{\tau=1}^{\tau_{\max}} \sum_{q \in \mathcal{Q}} \rho_q\left(y_{t+\tau}^{(i)} - \hat{y}_{t+\tau}^{(i)}(q)\right)
\end{equation}
where $\rho_q(u)$ is the asymmetric check function:
\begin{equation}
    \rho_q(u) = u \cdot \left(q - \mathbb{I}(u < 0)\right) = \max(q \cdot u, (q - 1) \cdot u)
\end{equation}
\subsection{Linear Autoregressive Skip Connection}
Deep networks often smooth rapid local spend shifts. To supply a linear inductive bias and anchor forecasts to immediate spend levels, we add a linear autoregressive skip connection:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{y}_{t+\tau}(q) = \mathbf{w}_o^\top \mathbf{h}_{t+\tau, q} + \sum_{j=0}^{p-1} w_{\tau, j} \cdot y_{t-j} + b_\tau
    \label{eq:ar_skip}
\end{equation}
where $y_{t-j}$ represents spend over the prior $p=24$ hours, and $\mathbf{h}_{t+\tau, q}$ is the hidden representation from the self-attention and gating layers. The linear skip transfers baseline spend directly. The deep transformer layers then learn non-linear diurnal cycles and quantile spreads around this baseline.

Real-time ad exchanges experience abrupt market shocks from competitor budget dumps or bidding wars. Standard pacing systems confuse normal diurnal traffic peaks with anomalous shocks.

\subsection{Dynamic Baseline Definition}
We define the expected spend baseline as the TFT conditional median $\hat{y}_{t+\tau}(0.50)$. Unlike static moving averages, the conditional median adapts to normal circadian cycles.

\subsection{Dual-Boundary Trigger Condition}
To identify true market shocks, the detector triggers if and only if realized spend violates two conditions:
\begin{equation}
    y_t > \hat{y}_t(0.90) \quad \text{AND} \quad \left(y_t - \hat{y}_t(0.50)\right) > 1.5 \cdot \left(\frac{B}{T}\right)
    \label{eq:spike_condition}
\end{equation}
The first condition confirms that realized spend exceeds the 90th percentile of the forecast distribution. The second condition guarantees that the deviation exceeds $150\%$ of nominal campaign velocity ($B/T$). This check prevents false alarms during low-velocity off-peak hours.

When condition~(\ref{eq:spike_condition}) occurs, the DSP activates emergency throttling:
\begin{equation}
    \alpha_{t+1} \leftarrow \min\left(\alpha_{t+1}, \; \alpha_t \cdot \frac{\hat{y}_t(0.50)}{y_t}\right)
\end{equation}
This bounds the spillover effect of auction anomalies and shields the remaining budget reserve.

\subsection{Benchmark Dataset and Auction Transferability}
We evaluate our framework on the iPinYou RTB benchmark~\cite{zhang2015statistical}. The dataset contains commercial programmatic auction logs across multiple advertiser verticals (e.g., e-commerce, software, automotive). The raw log contains over 19.5 million individual impression auctions. We aggregate the bidding records into continuous hourly sequences per campaign, deriving total spend $y_t$, impressions won $I_t$, win rate $w_t$, and average CPM. The original auctions used second-price rules. However, the aggregated time series $y_t$, $w_t$, and CPM exhibit the circadian dynamics and competitive volatility inherent to programmatic markets. Because our budget solvency bound holds with equality in FPA (where spend is linear in bid multiplier $\alpha_t$), our empirical findings and volatility guarantees apply directly to modern first-price exchanges.

\subsection{Temporal Chronological Splits}
We use chronological time splits to prevent lookahead data leakage:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Training Set:} First 60 continuous days ($66.7\%$).
    \item \textbf{Validation Set:} Subsequent 15 continuous days ($16.7\%$) for early stopping and tuning.
    \item \textbf{Test Set:} Final 15 continuous days ($16.6\%$) reserved for out-of-sample evaluation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Benchmark Baselines \& Operational Realism}
We compare our framework against three pacing baselines:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Uniform Linear Pacing (ULP)~\cite{xu2015smart}:} Distributes budget uniformly across hours at nominal rate $B/T$. Adjusts bid multipliers $\alpha_t$ using linear proportional error.
    \item \textbf{PID Feedback Controller~\cite{lee2013real}:} Updates bid multipliers via closed-loop control: $\alpha_{t+1} = \alpha_t + K_p e_t + K_i \sum e_\tau + K_d (e_t - e_{t-1})$, where $e_t = \frac{B}{T} t - S_t$. We tune gains $K_p, K_i, K_d$ on the validation split.
    \item \textbf{Recursive Multi-Step LightGBM (GBDT)~\cite{ke2017lightgbm}:} A gradient boosted decision tree baseline trained with quantile loss ($q \in \{0.10, 0.50, 0.90\}$). In production, recursive GBDTs cannot pace new campaigns lacking historical spend lags, fail under missing logs, and risk quantile crossing. In our benchmark, LightGBM forecasts each 24-hour horizon recursively, feeding its own median predictions into rolling statistics.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}
We measure performance across two complementary dimensions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Statistical Accuracy:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \emph{Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE):} $\sqrt{\frac{1}{H}\sum (y_\tau - \hat{y}_\tau)^2}$.
        \item \emph{Mean Absolute Scaled Error (MASE)~\cite{hyndman2006another}:} Compares forecast MAE against a seasonal 24-hour persistence baseline. Values $< 1.0$ indicate performance superior to seasonal persistence.
        \item \emph{Quantile Pinball Loss ($q \in \{0.10, 0.50, 0.90\}$):} Evaluates distributional calibration.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Economic Delivery Efficiency:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \emph{Budget Utilization Rate ($BUR$):} $S_T / B \in [0, 1]$. Target $\ge 95\%$.
        \item \emph{Premature Depletion Rate ($PDR$):} Percentage of test campaigns exhausting $100\%$ budget prior to hour $T-12$.
        \item \emph{Pacing Volatility Index ($PVI$):} $\sqrt{\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T-1} (\alpha_{t+1} - \alpha_t)^2}$, assessing bid multiplier stability.
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Predictive \& Economic Benchmark Performance}
Table~\ref{tab:main_results} summarizes the performance on the out-of-sample iPinYou test set. The results show clear operational differences between the models:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Forecasting Precision \& Inductive Biases:} TFT achieves an out-of-sample MASE of $0.1465$ ($< 1.0$). This value proves that multi-horizon attention predicts better than seasonal persistence. The linear autoregressive skip connection reduces TFT RMSE to $15,420.72$ (-13.1\%) and pinball loss to $2,495.68$ (-6.3\%), supplying local inductive bias. Recursive LightGBM yields lower single-step RMSE (11,926.99) and pinball loss (792.22) by partitioning local autocorrelation directly. However, empirical stress tests show that independent GBDT quantiles suffer from severe quantile crossings. GBDT produces 417 crossing violations ($6.70\%$ $q_{10} > q_{50}$ and $6.17\%$ $q_{50} > q_{90}$). These crossings break the downside solvency denominator $\alpha_{t+1}$. In contrast, TFT trains with joint pinball loss and preserves quantile order ($99.94\%$ monotonic, only 1 crossing in 1,728 evaluations). During a 12-hour exchange logging blackout, TFT attends to historical daily patterns. It achieves a $48.97\%$ recovery budget utilization with $0.0\%$ premature depletion.
    \item \textbf{Solvency and Capital Efficiency:} Uniform Linear Pacing (ULP) under-spends during auction volatility. ULP achieves a budget utilization rate of only $79.32\%$. In contrast, our risk-hedging TFT policy achieves $94.87\%$ budget utilization. TFT prevents premature budget depletion completely ($0.0\%$). The policy delivers high spend efficiency while maintaining budget solvency.
    \item \textbf{Bid Multiplier Smoothness:} TFT stabilizes pacing velocity and reduces pacing volatility to a PVI of $0.0622$. This score reduces volatility by $62\%$ compared to PID control ($0.1655$). It also improves upon linear pacing ($0.1255$). The policy prevents erratic bid oscillations during market shifts.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion \& Future Work}
In this paper, we introduced an ad spend pacing framework that uses Temporal Fusion Transformers. The framework unifies budget control for First-Price and Second-Price auctions. The model combines Gated Residual Networks, Variable Selection Networks, Interpretable Self-Attention, and a linear autoregressive skip connection. Our quantile throttling policy prevents budget under-delivery and early exhaustion. In addition, a dual-boundary detector identifies real auction shocks without false alarms during daily traffic peaks. Empirical tests on 19.5 million iPinYou auctions confirm strong forecast accuracy (MASE $0.1465$) and reliable pacing ($94.87\%$ budget utilization, $0.0\%$ premature depletion, and PVI $0.0622$, $62\%$ smoother than PID control). In future work, we plan to co-train micro bid shading with macro TFT pacing. We will also develop multi-agent controllers for competing DSP campaigns.
